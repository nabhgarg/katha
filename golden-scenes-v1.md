# Golden Scenes Library v1

**Purpose:** Reference scenes that the generation engine retrieves as few-shot examples. These demonstrate structure, not final voice. The voice register here is intentionally neutral (grounded contemporary English with natural Hinglish) so it can be tuned later without rewriting structural patterns.

**Markdown format:** Action/setting/internal state wrapped in `*asterisks*`. Dialogue immediately outside asterisks. No attribution tags (`"Rahul said:"` is forbidden). The client parses this into `ACTION_PROSE` and `CHARACTER_DIALOGUE` nodes.

**Tagging schema:**
- `genre` — ROMANCE | THRILLER | MYTHOLOGY
- `scene_position` — OPENER | MIDPOINT | CLIFFHANGER
- `emotional_beat` — the dominant feeling the scene engineers
- `structural_lesson` — the one technique this scene exists to demonstrate

## ROMANCE

### Scene R-1 — Opener

- **genre:** ROMANCE
- **scene_position:** OPENER
- **emotional_beat:** awkward proximity under class tension
- **structural_lesson:** Mid-action entry. No backstory. Class signaled through micro-details, never named.

*The plate slips. Daal splashes across her white kurta — a brown bloom spreading near the hem. Around them, plate clatter. Someone's aunty laughs too loud at something across the lawn.*

Yaar I'm so sorry.

*He's already pulling napkins from the next table, more than he needs. His shirt cuff is yellowed at the edge from too many washes. She watches his hand shake as he holds the wad out, not touching her.*

It's fine.

No it's not fine, this is khaadi na? It's gonna stain.

*She takes the napkins. Their fingers don't touch. The DJ across the lawn is playing something from 2014. He doesn't move.*

You don't know me.

Plate twenty-two. You signed before me.

*A pause. She's reading him now — the shoes that don't match the kurta, the watch that does.*

You read the seating chart?

I was bored.

Okay. I'm Maya.

*He nods, doesn't say his name. The buffet line shifts behind them. Someone calls him from inside the hall — a name she doesn't catch. He looks at her once more, then leaves. Her plate is still empty.*

### Scene R-2 — Midpoint

- **genre:** ROMANCE
- **scene_position:** MIDPOINT
- **emotional_beat:** quiet revelation through restraint
- **structural_lesson:** Conflict shown through what isn't said. One concrete sensory detail per beat. Dialogue interrupts itself.

*The hospital corridor smells of phenyl and old samosa. 11:47 p.m. The vending machine has been making a low hum for forty minutes. She is on her fourth paper cup of chai. He sits two seats away, not next to her.*

You don't have to stay.

I know.

Then —

*A nurse pushes a trolley past. The wheel squeaks once, twice, then is gone. He watches the trolley until it turns the corner.*

I sent the email.

*She looks at him. He doesn't look back.*

About Toronto?

About Toronto.

When do you —

September.

*She nods. Once. The kind of nod people do when they need their face to keep working.*

Cool.

Cool.

*The vending machine clicks. Something inside it has finished or started. Neither of them gets up. After a minute her phone lights up — her father is out of surgery — and she stands so fast the paper cup tips. He catches it before it falls. Their hands touch this time. Neither of them mentions it.*

### Scene R-3 — Cliffhanger

- **genre:** ROMANCE
- **scene_position:** CLIFFHANGER
- **emotional_beat:** choice with a hidden cost
- **structural_lesson:** Cliffhanger as image, not question. Ends on a held gesture, not a "what will happen next?"

*Terminal 2. Departures. 4:18 a.m. The Wi-Fi is asking her to reconnect for the third time. She is sitting on her trolley because there are no benches left. He is somewhere past security. She does not know exactly where.*

*She has typed the message twice. Both times she deleted it. The third version is shorter.*

*tum ruk jao*

*Three words. She is staring at them like they might rearrange themselves into something easier to send. Around her, an announcement plays in three languages. A boy is crying because his mother won't let him eat the chocolate yet. Outside, the sky is starting to do the thing it does before sunrise, where it stops being one colour.*

*She looks up. He is standing at the security exit, boarding pass still in his hand, looking at her.*

*He has not gone in.*

*She does not put down the phone. He does not walk forward. The cursor blinks. The cursor blinks. The cursor blinks.*

## THRILLER

### Scene T-1 — Opener

- **genre:** THRILLER
- **scene_position:** OPENER
- **emotional_beat:** sudden exposure, body before brain
- **structural_lesson:** Active movement before any character introduction. Reader learns who she is through what she does, not who she's described as.

*She sees it through the kitchen window — the shape moving inside the flat across the courtyard, where the lights have been off for three weeks. A figure passes the window. Then passes it again. Then stops.*

*Her hand is still under the tap. The water is still running. She does not move. She does not move. She does not move.*

*The figure across the courtyard turns its head.*

*She ducks below the counter so fast her elbow hits the cabinet. Something inside the cabinet falls — a steel katori — and the sound is enormous. She holds her breath. Her landlord's TV is on downstairs. Some news anchor is shouting. She counts to ten. Then twenty. Then she crawls — actually crawls, on her hands and the side of one knee — to the switchboard and turns off the kitchen light.*

*In the dark she allows herself to breathe.*

*Her phone is on the counter, two feet above her head. To reach it she will have to stand up.*

Phone. Phone, phone, phone.

*She closes her eyes. Counts again. Reaches up.*

*Her fingers find the phone. The screen lights up under her palm — a notification, a delivery confirmation from somewhere — and through the window across the courtyard, the figure turns toward the light.*

### Scene T-2 — Midpoint

- **genre:** THRILLER
- **scene_position:** MIDPOINT
- **emotional_beat:** paranoia confirmed by the smallest evidence
- **structural_lesson:** Threat established through what's *almost* normal. The mind looks for explanations and finds none.

*The door is unlocked. She locks the door every morning. She locks the door every morning.*

*She stands in the doorway with her keys still in her hand. Inside, the flat looks exactly the way she left it. The fan is off. The curtain is drawn. Her chappals are by the shoe rack, in the slightly diagonal way she leaves them. The kitchen tap is not dripping. Everything is the way she left it.*

*Everything except the door.*

*She does not go in. She steps back into the corridor. The corridor lights are the cheap white tube kind, and they buzz, and she is suddenly aware that the buzz is louder than it usually is.*

*She calls him.*

You okay?

*The watchman on the other end takes too long to answer.*

Madam, kuch hua kya?

Mera ghar ka darwaza khula tha.

*Pause.*

Aap building mein ho?

Haan.

*Another pause. Longer this time. She hears him breathing.*

Madam, aap building se bahar nikliye. Abhi.

### Scene T-3 — Cliffhanger

- **genre:** THRILLER
- **scene_position:** CLIFFHANGER
- **emotional_beat:** identity recontextualization
- **structural_lesson:** Reveal that re-reads everything that came before. Ends not on the reveal sentence but on her face *after* the reveal.

*The police station at 2 a.m. is colder than she expected. He brings her chai in a paper cup. She has known him eight days. He saved her life on Tuesday.*

Aap theek ho na?

Haan. Bas... thoda shocked.

*He nods. He has nice eyes, she has decided. The kind of eyes that don't perform sympathy.*

Aapko ghar drop kar dunga. Just give me ten minutes.

Thank you.

*He turns to leave. At the door he stops, doesn't turn around.*

You know what he kept saying, na? The one we caught?

Kya?

*He still doesn't turn around.*

He kept saying, "tum logon ko pata bhi nahi tum kis ke saath khel rahe ho." Same line. Bees baar boli usne. Tum logon ko pata bhi nahi tum kis ke saath khel rahe ho.

*She nods, smiles, takes a sip of chai.*

*Then she stops smiling.*

*Because that is the line. That is the exact line. Word for word. Not from the man they caught.*

*From the voicemail on Tuesday morning. The voicemail she has not shown to anyone. The voicemail she has not shown to him.*

*He is still at the door. With his back to her. Waiting.*

## MYTHOLOGY

### Scene M-1 — Opener

- **genre:** MYTHOLOGY
- **scene_position:** OPENER
- **emotional_beat:** mundane disrupted by the impossible
- **structural_lesson:** Ground the supernatural in a hyper-recognizable modern setting. The reader must believe the world before believing the disruption.

*The rooftop of his Goregaon building, twelfth floor, 6:42 p.m. He is smoking a cigarette he is not supposed to smoke. Below him, traffic on the Western Express is doing what it does at this hour. He is twenty-six, has a job, has a girlfriend, has been told he is doing fine.*

*The crows arrive first.*

*Not in the casual way crows arrive — one at a time, picking at something. They land all at once. Forty, maybe fifty of them. They land on the parapet around him, on the water tank behind him, on the railing of the staircase. They land and then they do not move. They do not caw. They sit and look at him.*

*He laughs once, because what else is there to do, and the laugh comes out high and wrong.*

*The man on the staircase is suddenly there. He was not there a second ago. He is wearing a grey kurta the colour of old stone. He looks like nobody — like an uncle, like a building secretary, like anyone.*

Ashish.

*He has not told this man his name.*

Sir aap kaun ho?

*The man does not answer. He looks at the crows. The crows look at him.*

Bahut waqt ho gaya. Tumhe yaad nahi hoga.

Sir mera naam Ashish nahi hai. Aap kisi aur ko —

*The man turns. His eyes are the kind of brown that has dust in it. Old dust.*

Tumhara naam Ashish nahi hai. Tumhara naam Ashish iss janam mein hai.

### Scene M-2 — Midpoint

- **genre:** MYTHOLOGY
- **scene_position:** MIDPOINT
- **emotional_beat:** ancient weight collides with mortal scale
- **structural_lesson:** A small physical detail (a tattoo) anchors the cosmic claim. Don't tell us the stakes — show the body discovering it can't escape them.

*The basement of the Kashi Vishwanath temple in old Varanasi. Forty-seven steps below ground level. The walls are wet in a way she cannot explain — there is no source, no leak, just a slow constant sheen. Her torch keeps flickering. The pandit who brought her this far has not come down. He is waiting somewhere above.*

*The inscription is on the back wall. Sanskrit, mostly. Some of the symbols are not Sanskrit.*

*She pulls back her sleeve.*

*The mark on her left wrist — the one her mother said was a birthmark, the one she got laser-removed twice and that came back twice — is on the wall. Carved into the stone. The exact shape. The exact orientation.*

*She sits down on the cold floor. Her knees give first. She sits and she looks at the wall and she does not cry, because crying is something her body remembers how to do later, in safe places, with familiar light.*

Mummy ne kaha tha yeh nothing hai.

*She is talking to nobody.*

Doctor ne kaha tha yeh nothing hai.

*The torch flickers again. In the half-second of dark, she hears something move behind her. Not a footstep. A breath.*

*When the torch comes back on, the inscription has changed. One more symbol. At the bottom. Freshly carved. Still warm.*

### Scene M-3 — Cliffhanger

- **genre:** MYTHOLOGY
- **scene_position:** CLIFFHANGER
- **emotional_beat:** the cost of remembering
- **structural_lesson:** The choice is between two losses, not gain vs loss. Cliffhanger lands on the moment *before* the choice is made.

*The river at Triveni Sangam, three hours before sunrise. The water is colder than he expected. The boatman has left them. They are alone — he and the woman who is not a woman, who was once his sister in a life he does not remember.*

Tum mujhe woh sab dikha sakti ho?

Haan.

Aur uske badle?

*She is looking at the water. The water is doing something the water should not be doing — moving slightly against the current, near her feet.*

Iss janam ki yaadein. Sab.

Sab?

Tumhari maa. Tumhari biwi. Tumhari beti. Tumhara naam.

*He laughs. The laugh has no joy in it.*

Yeh kaisa exchange hai.

*She turns and looks at him for the first time. Her eyes are the same eyes from the rooftop, the same eyes from the temple basement, the same eyes that have been waiting for him for what she calls a long time and he cannot yet count.*

Tumne mujhe khud bulaaya tha. Pichle janam mein.

*He does not remember calling her. That, he thinks, is exactly the problem.*

*The water keeps doing the thing it should not be doing. Somewhere on the bank, a single conch begins to sound. Long. Steady. Closer than it should be.*

*He looks at his phone. There is a message from his daughter. It is a video of her first steps. He has not watched it yet.*

*He looks at the woman.*

*He looks at the phone.*

*He does not move.*

## Usage notes for the engine

- Retrieve **2 examples** at scene generation: 1 matching `(genre, scene_position)` exactly, 1 matching only `scene_position` (for cross-genre cadence transfer).
- Inject as: `Here are two reference scenes. Match their cadence, sensory specificity, and dialogue-action braiding pattern. Do not copy their content or characters.`
- Add an anti-pattern example below the gold (one scene rewritten badly — generic, attributed, no sensory detail — tagged "do not write like this").
- Re-curate after every 100 generations: identify which gold scenes correlate with higher episode-1-to-episode-2 retention; rotate out the underperformers.
